## Reviewed mechanics

1. **Price semantics and update triggers.** A price feed exposes an aggregated observation through a proxy and an underlying aggregator. Updates are triggered by either configured offchain deviation or elapsed heartbeat, whichever occurs first. Heartbeat is a round trigger, not a delivery-time guarantee; congestion can delay publication. Record both actual `updatedAt` and the feed-specific published heartbeat/deviation settings. Wrapped assets and different networks may use different settings even for apparently similar pairs. Sources: [Data Feeds, monitoring and components](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds#check-the-timestamp-of-the-latest-answer); [Decentralized Data Model, Aggregator](https://docs.chain.link/architecture-overview/architecture-decentralized-model#aggregator).

2. **API fields and units.** `latestRoundData()` returns `roundId`, signed `answer`, `startedAt`, `updatedAt`, and deprecated `answeredInRound`; `decimals()` supplies precision and `description()` supplies the description. `latestAnswer()` alone lacks freshness information. Proxy `version()` and aggregator `typeAndVersion()` are different concepts. Validate the selected chain, proxy, pair, quote unit, answer type and actual contract behavior. A universal eight-decimal assumption is unsupported. Source: [API Reference, AggregatorV3Interface and AccessControlledOffchainAggregator](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/api-reference).

3. **Freshness and safe failures.** The consumer decides acceptable freshness and response to delays. Pausing, alternate operating modes, circuit breakers and independent comparison are application responsibilities. A readable positive answer can still be stale. Conversely, unchanged price is not by itself failed liveness. Absence of a feed or unreadable data should remain unknown/unavailable until understood. Sources: [Monitoring](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds#monitoring-data-feeds); [Developer Responsibilities, Application Code Risks](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/developer-responsibilities#application-code-risks). The treatment of unknown data is a research recommendation, not a rule supplied by Chainlink.

4. **Fallback is not a universal Chainlink guarantee.** The selecting-feeds guide calls for application-level fallback/pause controls, particularly for single-source dependencies. Reading through a proxy supports aggregator upgrades; it does not prove automatic substitution with an independent fresh price when the source fails. A proposed fallback needs source independence, asset/quote equivalence, units, freshness, activation logic, upgrade authority and recovery rules. Sources: [Risk Mitigation](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/selecting-data-feeds#risk-mitigation); [Single Source Data Providers](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/selecting-data-feeds#single-source-data-providers); [Proxy/aggregator updates](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds#updates-to-proxy-and-aggregator-contracts). The fallback verification checklist is an inference from those responsibilities.

5. **Feed integrity is broader than oracle consensus.** Standard market feeds have aggregation at source, node and network levels. Multiple operators do not eliminate correlated dependence on the same venue or an authoritative single publisher. The official sourcing table differentiates multi-vendor crypto market data from offchain reserve/NAV data supplied by custodians, administrators or asset managers and onchain exchange rates. Market concentration, thin depth, liquidity migration, manipulated trades and network outages can affect the economic usefulness of an otherwise correctly relayed observation. Sources: [Data Sources](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/data-sources); [Evaluating Data Sources and Risks](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/selecting-data-feeds#evaluating-data-sources-and-risks).

6. **Underlying, wrapped and redeemable values differ.** An underlying-asset feed can continue reporting a valid underlying price while a wrapped/bridged representation loses convertibility. Exchange-rate feeds report protocol redemption rates, not necessarily secondary-market liquidation prices. A reserve observation does not independently establish an investor's legal title, complete liabilities, redemption eligibility or execution liquidity. Sources: [Wrapped or Bridged Assets](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/selecting-data-feeds#evaluating-wrapped-or-bridged-assets); [Exchange Rate Feeds](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/selecting-data-feeds#exchange-rate-feeds); [Data Sources](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/data-sources). The legal/exit distinction is an analytical inference.

7. **Bounds must be identified by implementation.** General aggregator `minAnswer`/`maxAnswer` fields are unused on most feeds, so their presence is not a working circuit breaker. Separately, current documentation describes bounded market price feeds: a stablecoin price above the configured upper bound is not written; readers receive the last published answer. This is different from returning the cap as the price and differs again from a protocol's own CAPO adapter. The guide describes no lower bound for that stablecoin feed category. Therefore age and bound events must be interpreted together, using actual feed metadata and code. Sources: [Reasonable limits](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds#check-the-latest-answer-against-reasonable-limits); [Bounded Market Price Feeds](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/selecting-data-feeds#bounded-market-price-feeds); [Stablecoin considerations](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/selecting-data-feeds#stablecoin-specific-considerations).

8. **L2 sequencer monitoring is separate from a price feed.** Chainlink lists Base Mainnet sequencer proxy `0xBCF85224fc0756B9Fa45aA7892530B47e10b6433`. The uptime answer uses 0 for up and 1 for down; `startedAt` records the status transition. Its example waits through a recovery grace period. The example's 3,600 seconds and OP addresses are examples, not Base/Aave parameters. Source documentation says a zero uninitialized `startedAt` case is Arbitrum-specific. A sequencer feed does not prove the Aave deployment actually consults it. Sources: [L2 Sequencer Uptime Feeds, BASE and Example Consumer Contract](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/l2-sequencer-feeds); [Official example code](https://github.com/smartcontractkit/documentation/blob/main/public/samples/DataFeeds/DataConsumerWithSequencerCheck.sol).

9. **History and upgrades.** Proxy round identifiers encode aggregator phase and round identity; aggregator changes create boundaries that historical collectors must handle. Preserve proxy, underlying aggregator, phase, block number/hash, timestamp and raw answer for reproducibility. Source: [Getting Historical Data](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/historical-data). The evidence record is a recommendation derived from these mechanics.

10. **Categories, market hours and deprecation.** Official categories describe market-pricing risk, not an insurance promise or a numeric failure probability. Unrated is not equivalent to low risk. A trading-hours feed needs its stated calendar and delay policy. The current shutdown policy says `latestAnswer` returns zero and `latestRoundData` reverts after shutdown; notice is ordinarily two weeks but can be accelerated. These are documented policies that require monitoring for revisions. Source: [Selecting Quality Data Feeds, categories, market hours and shutdown](https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/selecting-data-feeds).

## Candidate observations, without scoring rules

| Observable | Evidence needed | Interpretation boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Answer age and missed-update episodes | Raw round timestamps, block time, per-feed heartbeat, chain congestion history | Heartbeat is not a hard delivery SLA; do not use one global cutoff. |
| Difference from an independent executable reference | Same asset, denomination, timestamp and attainable trade size | Difference may reflect venue microstructure, stale reference or asset mismatch. |
| Feed/adapter source graph | Proxy, aggregator, adapter code, reserve source mapping, governance changes | Brand name alone cannot establish independence or fallback. |
| L2 availability and recovery | Uptime feed, chain status evidence, actual consumer grace configuration | RPC reachability is not sequencer safety or finality. |
| Bounds, caps and fallback transitions | Verified code, configured bounds, events and historical prices | Different bounds have different semantics; avoid treating suppressed writes as ordinary stable pricing. |
| Source concentration and market depth | Primary venue/orderbook/pool data and official feed methodology | Node count does not establish market-source diversity. |
| Deprecation or implementation changes | Official notices, proxy changes, effective times | Missing historical metadata cannot be treated as unchanged configuration. |
